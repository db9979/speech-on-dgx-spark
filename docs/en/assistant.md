# Assistant and panel

← [README](../../README.md)

## Voice chat

The **Gespräch** tab needs the microphone, and browsers only allow it over https. The panel therefore also listens on `https://SPARK:31443` with a self-signed certificate; the browser warns once. The LLM connection is under Settings → Language model (default: qwen38 at `http://127.0.0.1:30001/v1`). The installer takes the qwen38 API key from `~/.config/qwen38/api-key` of the user who runs `sudo ./install.sh`. Thinking is off for the chat so the answer starts right away. An animated assistant face shows whether it is listening, thinking or speaking; its mouth follows the voice. The same face sits in the bottom right corner of every tab, so you can talk from anywhere in the panel. Since V01.0.154 the admin picks under Settings → Defaults → "Gesicht des Assistenten" between *Robot* (as before, the default) and *Comic* (a caricature with spiky hair and a mustache). The comic face looks around, blinks with heavy lids, raises its brows, tilts its head, moves mouth and mustache with the voice and shows the state with a coloured ring: grey idle, blue listening, yellow thinking, green speaking, red error. The choice applies to everybody, the iPhone app included; more faces can be added in `static/js/face.js`.

**Start page and password:** the panel opens with the assistant. Monitoring, settings, voices and logs need the panel password (button "Settings"; the login ends after 7 days without use and renews itself while in use). "Assistant without password" under Settings → Security is off on new installs: then guests are locked out, profiles sign in on the start page with name and PIN (devices with a key keep working), and the admin signs in with "Admin". Switched on, the assistant is open to everyone on the network, also without a profile. The password can be changed under Settings → Security. Scripts can still use HTTP Basic.

**On the phone:** below 760 px the page opens with the big face (tap = talk) and two buttons below it: "Settings" opens the "Ich" window ("Hey Spark" at the top), "History" the messenger view. That view fits the screen exactly: a slim top bar with an arrow back to the face, a small face, state, profile and menu, the history in the middle, and at the bottom a text field and round mic button (a stop button during the answer). The browser remembers which of the two views was open last. The menu (☰) holds new conversation, earlier conversations, conversation settings, language, light/dark and, for admins, every tab.

**As an app on your phone:** open `https://SPARK:31443` in the phone's browser, accept the certificate warning, then use "Add to Home screen" in the browser menu (iPhone: Safari → Share → "Add to Home Screen"). The assistant then starts full screen with its own icon. With the self-signed certificate Android creates a shortcut instead of an installed app; it works the same, it just does not show up in the app list.

**Better recognition:** a fixed default language (Settings → Speech recognition, e.g. German) instead of "auto" helps most with short sentences. The "Context" field takes names and terms that are often misheard; the model then prefers them. The 1.7B model is noticeably more accurate than 0.6B and needs about 2–3 GiB more memory.

**Second recognizer: Parakeet.** Under Settings → Speech recognition → "Recognizer" the admin switches between Qwen3-ASR (GPU) and Parakeet ([primeline/parakeet-primeline](https://huggingface.co/primeline/parakeet-primeline), a German fine-tune of NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3; the download is [flozen1981/parakeet-primeline-onnx](https://huggingface.co/flozen1981/parakeet-primeline-onnx), an int8 ONNX export of it, run via sherpa-onnx; idea from [winidi/dictate](https://github.com/winidi/dictate)). Parakeet runs on the CPU and needs about 1 GiB; the Qwen3-ASR engine is stopped meanwhile. It ignores the context field, and a stream returns the whole text in one piece. The first start downloads about 640 MB.

**Conversation comfort:** the assistant knows the date and time (the browser's time zone; can be turned off under Settings → Language model). Conversations are kept in the browser and can be reopened or deleted at the top of the conversation. With "live transcript" the text appears while you speak; in the pause the recognition is usually already done, so that step is skipped at the end.

**Guides:** every service that can be switched on has an openable guide (what it does, what you need, setup, example phrases, what leaves the house, switching off, troubleshooting): next to its switch under Settings → Features, at the top of its page in the "Ich" window and collected under Integrate → Guides. Services are sorted into the same groups everywhere: Conversation, My day, Mail and documents, Home, On the go and devices, Security and access. The texts live in `app/panel/static/js/guides.js`; a self-test checks that no switch is left without a guide and group.

**Weather, contacts, parcels (V01.0.93):** three small services, each off by default, allowed by the admin under Settings → Features and switched on by each profile itself in the "Ich" window; never guests. *Weather* comes straight from Open-Meteo (no key, only the place's coordinates leave, own address configurable); the Spark builds fixed sentences from the numbers, also for the daily briefing and the proactive weather note. *Contacts* reads an address book over CardDAV (iCloud: https://contacts.icloud.com with an app-specific password, Nextcloud, others) once a day and keeps it encrypted: phone numbers, e-mail addresses, birthdays (in the briefing and as a notice), names instead of addresses in mail. Contact text counts as outside text. *Parcels* recognises shipping mails of DHL, Hermes, DPD, GLS, UPS and Amazon in the profile's own mailbox (14 days) and says what comes today, is on its way, waits for pick-up or was delivered; no carrier is asked, parcel lines count as mail text.

**Telegram (V01.0.96):** your own bot from @BotFather (guide in the panel). The admin switches it on under Settings → Features and enters the token (stored encrypted); the Spark asks Telegram for new messages itself, no open port needed. Each profile links its Telegram in the "Ich" window with a one-time code (10 minutes) and writes or sends voice messages (speech recognition on the Spark). Strangers get one fixed sentence, groups nothing. Per profile, all off at first: answers also as voice messages, notifications over Telegram, personal data (calendar, mail, contacts, parcels, documents; also appointments and mail in notifications) and switching the smart home, only with the code word. Commands: /neu, /stimme, /trennen.

**Own speakers (ESP32, V01.0.99, without display since V01.0.104):** small ESP32-S3 boards (16 MB flash, PSRAM, INMP441 microphone, MAX98357A amplifier, e.g. the XiaoZhi breadboard boards) become a voice assistant in the room. Firmware: the open XiaoZhi firmware at a fixed version, German, wake word "Jarvis", without a built-in cloud address; a GitHub Action (`.github/workflows/firmware.yml`, settings in `firmware/xiaozhi.json`) builds it as release `fw-<version>`. The Spark fetches the newest one daily (or on a button), checks every file against `manifest.json` and keeps it with the previous one. Set up in the "Ich" window → Speakers: over USB from Chrome or Edge (Web Serial, esptool-js 0.5.7 ships with the panel) the browser writes firmware and a settings block (Wi-Fi, Spark address, device key); the Wi-Fi password never reaches the Spark. Without a PC a board shows or says a 6-digit code. Each speaker is a device key of its profile (voice, memory, smart home with code word, room). Updates arrive over Wi-Fi when the board starts (per device "auto" can be switched off) or over USB. Admin switch `chat.esp32` and profile switch, both off at first; the reverse proxy must pass WebSockets. Board variants: no display, OLED 128×32, OLED 128×64, colour display, since V01.0.133 the Waveshare ESP32-S3-AUDIO-Board (round board with ES8311 audio chip and two microphones; display and camera are optional there). When a board is rewritten over USB with another variant, updates follow the board type it reports at start; the no-display variant never touches the display pins and, since firmware 2.5.1.3 (V01.0.132), no longer loads font and emoji either, which made it crash right after joining the Wi-Fi. Start check only by POST of at most 16 KB, at most 3 open codes per address, firmware only from github.com downloads with a size limit.

**Checking a speaker (V01.0.131):** under Me → Speakers every speaker has a *Check* button. It shows whether the admin and profile switches are on, whether there is firmware for the board variant, whether the board ever checked in, which address is in the board (with a warning for `https://<IP>` or port 31443, whose self-signed certificate the board refuses, for `localhost` and for a missing address) and what the speaker did lately: start check, connection, refused connection with its reason, wake word, microphone (length, loudest moment and mean in dB, whether there was speech), what was understood and how much sound went back. These lines stay in memory only (40 per speaker, gone when the panel restarts). *Check network* asks the address from the Spark the way the board does: `GET /api/esp32/ping` (answers with a marker of this Spark) and a WebSocket with `x-spark-probe: 1`; this shows whether the address leads to this Spark, whether the certificate is accepted, whether the reverse proxy redirects or blocks WebSockets. Allowed like Home Assistant (home network yes, the Spark's own service ports no), only these two paths, answers of at most 4 KB, six checks in ten minutes per profile. Behind a router without NAT loopback the check can fail although the board reaches the Spark. *Test: sound and microphone* plays the soft tone and a test sentence at the next "Jarvis" or button press (at once when connected) and then checks the microphone with the next sentence: the speaker says what it understood, or that it heard nothing. *Read the board's log (USB)* connects the board in Chrome or Edge, restarts it and reads for 45 seconds what the firmware reports; the page translates the known lines (Wi-Fi joined or not, certificate refused, Spark unreachable, status 403, pairing code, conversation connection, crash, brownout, sound error). The text stays in the browser, words like token or password are blacked out; *Copy* takes it along for a bug report. Since V01.0.136 the list under *Check* follows live (every 2 seconds while the page is visible); in room mode it only says that a sentence was heard and whether it speaks in a pause, never what was heard. Since V01.0.135 every speaker has a *Volume* slider under Me → Speakers (0 to 100; the Spark sends it at every connection through the firmware's MCP tool `self.audio_speaker.set_volume`, at once when connected) and a *Microphone* choice less sensitive / normal / more sensitive (from which level the Spark takes sound as speech: about −41, −47 or −53 dB). Since V01.0.134 a quiet level from about −47 dB (and three times above the background noise) counts as speech, quiet recordings are raised up to 20 times before speech recognition, and every question writes a line "esp32: microphone at …" with length and level to the log. The log now also shows the start checks ("esp32: start check from …") and the reason of every refused connection. Since V01.0.137 the Spark buffers the sound: it starts only when 0.6 seconds are ready, keeps at most 0.9 seconds in flight (since V01.0.138: 0.7) (the board holds 1.2 seconds) and after a stall collects 0.3 seconds again instead of sending single snippets. When speech output could not keep up, *Check* shows "Ton stockte N-mal" and the log "esp32: sound stalled"; if it stutters without that line, the board's Wi-Fi is the cause. Since V01.0.138 (firmware 2.5.1.5) the board no longer drops sound when its buffer is full but waits; the connection then slows the Spark down. Before, Wi-Fi delays added up in long answers, the buffer overflowed and the gaps grew towards the end. The Spark's lead is lowered to 0.7 seconds so old firmware has more room too. The board fetches the new firmware itself at its next start (unplug it briefly).

**Speaker page (V01.0.149):** Me → Speakers shows the switch "Own speakers for me" at the top, then "My speakers" (one line per speaker with its state: connected, room mode until, update available) and "Add a speaker" with two ways: *Set up a new board over USB* (Chrome/Edge on a PC) and *Board shows a code*. Tapping a speaker opens its page in sections: **Sound** (volume, microphone), **Room mode** ("Listen now" as a Start/Stop button, duration, how much it may say, *What it may offer* with the same seven kinds as in the browser, stored per speaker on the Spark, room in Home Assistant, detect more, and with "Ignore TV voices" also who it listens to and trial), **Voice** (teach "My voice through this speaker"), **Firmware** (version, update now, updates by themselves, rewrite the firmware over USB), **Check** (checks with a readable board name, check network, sound and microphone test, live recent events, board log over USB) and *Remove speaker* at the bottom. Admin: under Features → Own speakers the firmware state comes first, then the address with *Check address* (tried from the Spark like "Check network", admin only, at most 6 checks in 10 minutes). Under Profiles and devices and Me → Security speaker keys are marked "Speaker, managed under Me → Speakers"; they cannot be moved to another profile.

**Tasks and shopping list (V01.0.96):** "Put milk on the shopping list", "What is on my to-do list?". Admin switch and profile switch, both off at first. Each of the two lists lives either on the Spark, where an iPhone shortcut with the device key fetches new entries (`POST /api/tasks/inbox?list=einkauf`, header `X-Speech-Device`; a GET only reads and changes nothing) into the Reminders app, or in a CalDAV task list of a calendar account (Nextcloud, mailbox.org, Radicale). Adding happens at once, ticking off and deleting by voice only after "Yes". After outside text the assistant adds nothing in the same answer.

**Bus and train (V01.0.102):** "When does the next bus leave?", "How do I get to Karlsruhe at 8?". Admin switch and profile switch, both off at first. Timetable from transport.rest (default https://v6.db.transport.rest, own address configurable); fixed sentences with delay, platform and cancellations. With a commute (destination, time, days) "Proactive" speaks up 45 to 5 minutes before when the connection is 5 or more minutes late or cancelled. Timetable text counts as outside text.

**Agent functions (V01.0.175):** the assistant works longer for you. Admin: Settings → Features → "Agent functions" on and pick a level per profile: *off* (default), *read and report only* or *also act after "Yes"*. The profile then switches it on itself under Me → Jobs. Never guests, other voices or ESP32 speakers.
- *Background jobs:* "Research which heat pump fits the pool and let me know." The job runs in the background (search, read pages, with "my data" also appointments, reminders and documents), then a notification comes by browser, iPhone app and Telegram (as set under Me → Notifications). The full report is under Me → Jobs and in the log, on request as a document under "My documents" (switch "Also keep every report under "Documents"" or "As document" at the job). Limits: one job at a time on the Spark, it waits while someone talks to the assistant; 8 rounds, 4 searches, 5 pages, 12 tool calls, 15 minutes, 20,000 characters; 3 waiting and 20 jobs per day and profile. A job only has reading tools, its report counts as outside text and is never learned.
- *Scheduled jobs:* "Sum up my appointments of the week every Monday at 7." The assistant reads the plan back in fixed words, it is saved only after your "Yes". Once, daily, weekdays, weekly or monthly, at most 10, pause and delete under Me → Jobs. If the Spark was off for more than 3 hours at the planned time, that run is skipped.
- *Routines (level "also act" only):* "Remember as routine Evening: terrace light on, pool to 36 degrees." After "Yes", later "Evening" is enough. Every step is a Home Assistant sentence and runs like a spoken command (same allowlist, same code word); at most 8 steps and 20 routines.
- *Services over MCP:* below "Agent functions" the switch "Services over MCP". The admin enters name, address (Streamable HTTP) and token (login plus a fresh code; token in the vault, never sent back). Every tool is off until the admin sets it to "read" or "act", and the profiles are ticked per service. Reading answers count as outside text; acting tools only propose and run after the "Yes". If a service changes a tool's description or inputs, it is off again after "Fetch tools again". The Spark never starts a program itself; home network addresses only with the permission under Security.

**Home Assistant Assist over Wyoming (V01.0.100):** Home Assistant uses the Spark's speech recognition and voice (Voice PE, HA app, ESPHome speakers). Admin: Settings → Features → "Home Assistant Assist (Wyoming)" on and enter Home Assistant's address (Wyoming has no password; with no address listed the Spark answers nobody). Since V01.0.106 the field suggests the address from a profile's Home Assistant setup when switched on (home network addresses only; behind a reverse proxy enter the real HA address by hand) and shows addresses refused lately. In HA: integration "Wyoming Protocol", host = the Spark, port 31003; in the voice assistant pick "spark" for speech-to-text and text-to-speech. The service runs inside the panel, so it needs the full install.

**Weather place:** postcode and place ("76307 Karlsbad") work; the name is searched in Germany and the postcode compared. When several places fit, the others can be picked; coordinates ("48.87, 8.50") work too.

**Web search:** switch on "Websuche" under Settings → Features and enter the address of your SearXNG instance right below it. If SearXNG's `settings.yml` allows `json` under `search: formats:`, the panel uses the JSON API, otherwise it reads the normal results page; "Test connection" shows whether it works. The LLM then searches by itself when a question needs current information (the LLM server needs tool calling; qwen38 has it on). The assistant says it is looking it up, reads the top pages and shows the sources under the answer. Since V01.0.130 the search is never silently missing: when it is off or has no address, the assistant says so, and the log has a line "chat: web search …" for every question (offered, off, no address, locked). Asking in so many words ("such im Internet", "google mal", "recherchier", "schau online nach", "search the web") always goes through the search. After reading a mail the search stays locked for the rest of that answer (a mail could otherwise carry its content to the web). When the very next question clearly needs the web, the panel leaves the mail answer out of that turn and searches; for other questions the assistant says why it does not search right now.

**Wake word "Hey Spark":** with the checkbox below the chat the assistant keeps listening. Say "Hey Spark" and it listens; "Hey Spark, what's the weather?" in one go is taken as the question. The browser detects speech bursts, the speech recognition on the Spark checks for the phrase, nothing goes to the internet. The screen stays on; on a phone keep the app in the foreground.

**The "Ich" (me) window:** since V01.0.125 in the same groups as the Features page (conversation, my data, my day, mail and contacts, home, on the go and devices, access); on a computer it starts with an *Overview* (the state of each page, e.g. "2 calendars connected"), on a phone the state sits right below each entry of the list. Notifications and the daily briefing have their own page *Notifications*. the button with your name (or "Guest") at the top of the history and the settings icon below the face open the same window. On the left is a short list: Conversation and, logged in, Memory, Log and Security, plus, depending on the features switched on, Documents, Calendar, E-mail, Smart home, Voice, Von selbst (speaking up by itself) and Raum-Modus (room mode); as a guest it shows "Sign in". Conversation holds listening (stop on silence, live transcript, barge-in and its sensitivity), answer (voice, profiles only; speaking rate, answer length) and display. Guests always hear the default voice. Logged in, the settings live in the profile on the Spark and apply on every device, including speakers with a device key; as a guest only in the browser. "Hey Spark" is set per device because it keeps the microphone and screen awake. Defaults for guests and new profiles are under Settings → Defaults. The speaking rate keeps the pitch; it also works for other apps that send `speed` to the TTS API (vllm-omni backend).

**Own documents:** logged-in profiles upload their own files in the "Ich" window (PDF with a text layer, Word, text, Markdown, HTML, CSV; up to 20 MB each). When asked about them, the assistant searches them and names the document below the answer. The search is keyword-based on the Spark, no extra model. Guests cannot upload anything, and no profile sees another's documents. Can be turned off under Settings → Features.

**Messages in the conversation:** hovering over a message (on a phone: tapping it) shows a bin and, on answers, a speaker. The bin deletes the message, a question together with its answer. The speaker reads the answer out again.

**Timers and reminders:** "remind me about the oven in 10 minutes" or "remind me tomorrow at 8 about the appointment". Pending reminders show as small chips below the toggles (✕ cancels); "which timers are running?" and "cancel the oven timer" work by voice too. When one is due, the page chimes, shows it in the conversation and speaks it; if the tab is in the background, a browser notification appears as well. This works while the page is open. Profiles keep their reminders on the Spark, guests only in their own browser. Since V01.0.176 a profile's reminder rings only once: the first device that plays it (an open page, the iPhone app or push) takes it on the Spark, all others stay silent. While a page of the profile is open, push waits 20 seconds so the reminder does not ring there and arrive as a notification too. A reminder that was more than 10 minutes overdue when the page opens shows quietly in the conversation ("Missed"). The iPhone app without Apple push rings with its own alarms; if it is closed then, it reports that the next time it opens. At the same moment as an open page it can then still ring twice; with Apple push it does not. Can be turned off under Settings → Features.

**Reminders as notifications** (profiles only): in the "Ich" window under "Conversation", "Reminders on this device → Switch on" turns on push notifications. A reminder then arrives also when the page is closed, and only on the devices of that profile. This needs https with a valid certificate (e.g. behind a reverse proxy); on an iPhone first add the page to the home screen ("Share → Add to Home Screen"). The Spark only sends to the browsers' push services (Apple, Google, Mozilla, Microsoft), encrypted. Since V01.0.181 every notification (reminder, "speaking up" note, morning briefing, agent report) arrives once: on the device the profile used last (an open page, the iPhone app or Telegram, at most 12 hours back), else the iPhone app, else Telegram, else the browser set up last. Private notes go over Telegram only when the profile allowed personal data there.

**Daily briefing as a notification** (profiles only): below that, each profile sets a time under "Tagesbriefing als Mitteilung" (daily briefing as notification). Every morning at that time a short briefing (appointments, reminders, unread mail, your topics) then goes to all devices of the profile with notifications on, and it is also kept in the history. "Aus" (off) turns it off.

**Daily briefing and calendar:** say "good morning" or "what's on today?" and the assistant reads out today's and tomorrow's appointments, today's reminders and a few lines on your own topics (e.g. "weather Berlin", via your SearXNG). "What do I have on Friday?" works too. Each profile connects one or more calendars (up to 8) in the "Ich" window, tab Calendar; an appointment found in two calendars is read out once. Each one is a CalDAV address with user and app password (iCloud: https://caldav.icloud.com with the Apple ID and an app-specific password from appleid.apple.com; Nextcloud: …/remote.php/dav; Radicale …), or a subscription link with webcal://, webcals:// or https:// (Google: "secret address in iCal format", a public iCloud calendar). New appointments: "put dentist on Tuesday 10 am in my calendar" makes the assistant read out a proposal; only when you answer with a plain yes in the same conversation does the panel itself write it to the first CalDAV calendar (optionally with an alarm). Subscription links stay read only. The password stays on the Spark and is never shown again. Guests get no calendar. Can be turned off under Settings → Features.

**Reading e-mail:** "Any new mail?", "What does Anna write?", "Read me the mail from the bank". Each profile connects up to 4 mailboxes over IMAP in the "Ich" window, tab E-mail: iCloud (imap.mail.me.com, your iCloud mail address and a new app-specific password from appleid.apple.com), Gmail (imap.gmail.com, app password, needs 2-step verification), GMX and web.de (allow IMAP in their settings first) or any other IMAP server with TLS. Outlook/Microsoft 365 is not supported yet (needs OAuth). The assistant only reads the inbox of the last 30 days and changes nothing: the mailbox is opened read only, no mail is sent, deleted, moved or marked as read. It summarizes mails and reads them out word for word only when asked; the daily briefing names the unread ones. Mail text is treated as someone else's words: once the assistant read a mail in an answer, in that answer it cannot switch smart home devices, change memory, cancel reminders, search the web or enter appointments, so a crafted mail cannot trigger anything; answers made from mails are not learned into memory. Guests and another person's voice at a different device get no access. The password is stored encrypted on the Spark and never shown again. Off until turned on under Settings → Features.

**Inbox tidying** (Settings → Features → "Postfach aufräumen", off by default; needs reading e-mail; profiles only): the Spark sorts advertising, newsletters, invoices, notifications and social media mail into folders under a main folder (`Spark/` unless renamed, e.g. `Spark/Werbung`; renamed in the "Ich" window under E-mail, empty puts the folders at the top. Renaming also renames them in the mailbox with IMAP RENAME, the mail moves along; if the new name exists already, the old folder stays) so the inbox keeps only what matters. It only moves; it never deletes, never sends and never marks mail as read. When the server has no IMAP MOVE (iCloud), it copies the mail into the target folder, checks that the copy arrived, and only then removes exactly that original from the inbox (UIDPLUS); no other mail is touched. Each profile picks per mailbox in the "Ich" window, tab E-mail, part "Postfach aufräumen": off, preview (only shows what it would move; a week is recommended to start), safe automatic (moves only what a rule or clear headers such as List-Unsubscribe prove) or fully automatic (also when the model is at least 85 % sure). Only new mail is checked, every 5, 15 (default) or 60 minutes. Who decides, in this order: people you wrote to (sent folder), sign-in and security mails and your "always keep" list stay put; then your rules (sender or @domain); then fixed headers; only then the language model, which may only return a category from the fixed list and has no tools. It asks first for invoices and notifications and for unclear mail; your answer can become a rule. Move a mail back into the inbox and it is not moved again, and the Spark asks. Brakes: at most 200 mails per run, and a run that would move more than half of at least 10 new mails stops and puts everything into the preview. Every move stays in the history for 30 days and can be undone. "Old mail" proposes once where the last 90 days belong; nothing moves until you press "Move". By voice: "Tidy my inbox", "Move everything from Lidl to advertising", "Undo that"; the assistant reads out a proposal and the panel carries it out only after your plain "yes". **Reply drafts** (per mailbox): "Write Anna that I'm coming" saves a draft in the drafts folder after your "yes"; you send it yourself in your mail app. Guests and other people's voices get no access.

**Room mode** (Settings → Features → "Raum-Modus", off by default; profiles only): the switch "Raum" below the face lets the assistant listen to the conversation in the room at this device for a chosen time (15 minutes to 4 hours, it ends by itself); an orange dot shows it. In a pause it helps when a fixed cue came up: an open question (with web search, nothing when in doubt), an appointment ("Shall I enter it?"), "cold", "too warm" or "too dark" (the room's real temperature from Home Assistant, offering to change the heating by one degree or switch on the light) and things to buy (Home Assistant shopping list). It changes something only after a "yes", for the smart home with the code word when one is set. In the "Ich" window under "Raum-Modus" each device sets the duration, how much it may say (questions only, also hints, also comments), each kind on its own, the device's room in Home Assistant and "text only" (silently in the conversation instead of spoken). What it hears stays in the panel's memory for five minutes and is never stored; room mode uses neither memory, mail nor earlier conversations. Everyone in the room should know it is listening. Since V01.0.95 it also offers a timer ("The pizza needs 12 more minutes") and a reminder ("I must not forget to call grandma"), both only after a "yes", and calculates unit questions ("How much is 180 degrees in Fahrenheit?"). A question that points back ("When did he die?") is completed from the last sentences, only with words that were said. With a taught voice and speaker identification on, only the profile's own voice can say "yes". A soft tone comes before it speaks; "Stopp" ends the sentence, "Nicht jetzt" keeps it quiet for 15 minutes, "Raummodus aus" (also "Hör auf zuzuhören") switches it off, from any voice. Since V01.0.126 the profile's quiet hours apply (Ich → Von selbst, 22 to 7 by default): then everything appears as text only, never spoken. When the page is in the background or the phone locked for more than two minutes (the microphone hears nothing there anyway), room mode ends with its summary; closing the tab ends it at once. Since V01.0.128 it also runs on the own ESP32 speakers, all board variants: "Raummodus an" after the wake word or the switch "Room" under Me → Speakers (when the speaker sleeps, it starts at its next wake word), where duration, how much it may say and the room in Home Assistant are set per speaker. The Spark keeps the connection open, cuts out every sentence, runs it through speech recognition and speaks in a pause after the soft tone; boards with a display show the sentence. It ends after its time, with "Raummodus aus", the switch, when the connection drops or when the admin or the profile switch it off; then the board waits for its wake word again. In quiet hours the speaker stays silent. Since V01.0.140 the speaker cuts long stretches of talk after at most 8 seconds and checks every sentence on its own, speaks after 1.5 seconds of quiet (counted from the last loud sound, not from the end of a piece), streams the speech, and each speaker has "detect more". Questions are now recognized without a question mark from the speech recognition ("Wie hoch ist die Zugspitze"), after "Sag mal" or "Weißt du, wann …" and with "Stimmt es, dass …"; questions about the people in the room ("Wo ist meine Brille") stay out. The answer is looked up while people still talk and is ready in the pause (in the browser too). "Also comments" is chattier: at most every two minutes, after three seconds of quiet, one casual sentence about the conversation. Since V01.0.142 Me → Speakers has "Stimme hier anlernen" (speaker identification on, browser login, with the second step when set up): the speaker asks for three sentences and keeps its own voiceprint for its microphone; a sentence counts for a profile when it fits any of its voiceprints (browser or speaker). Until somebody taught a voice through a speaker, it listens to everybody despite "only known voices" (log: "listens to everybody (no voice taught through it yet)"), because a voice taught in the browser is rarely recognized through the small board microphone. At most two speakers at once, because speech recognition runs all the time. While it speaks, the wake word interrupts it. Option "Genauer erkennen": the model looks for missed questions, appointments and shopping, counted only with a word-for-word quote. When room mode ends, the page shows what it said and did (not what it heard), not stored. Every decision writes a "room:" line to the log (why it spoke or stayed silent, never the heard text).

**Ignoring TV voices** (since V01.0.139; Settings → Features → Room mode → "Fernsehstimmen überhören", off by default): every device picks under Me → Room mode "Who it listens to" (speakers under Me → Speakers): *everybody* (as before, default), *only known voices while the TV is on* (recommended) or *always only known voices*. A voice is known when a profile taught it under Me → Speaker identification; speaker identification must be on. It then runs for every sentence in room mode, on the CPU next to speech recognition (about 0.1 s per sentence), with no extra GPU load. Whether the TV is on comes from Home Assistant at most every 30 seconds: a media player on, playing or buffering in the device's room, without a room set anywhere in the home; when Home Assistant does not answer, it listens to everybody as before. A sentence in a foreign voice triggers no question or suggestion and is dropped at once; the model never sees it, not with "Genauer erkennen" or comments either. Always counted: a sentence starting with "Spark" (also from visitors), "Stopp", "Nicht jetzt" and "Raummodus aus". A "yes" still counts only in the profile's voice. Very short sentences (under about one second of speech) are often not recognized and count as foreign. With *Trial* (on at first) nothing is ignored, only counted: at the end the summary and the log say "Probelauf: Von 40 Sätzen hätte ich 12 als fremde Stimme überhört.", for speakers also under *Check*. Only this number is kept, never the text or whose voice it was; the log says "room: ignored (unknown voice)" or "(unknown voice, tv on)".

**Speaking up by itself** (Settings → Features → "Von selbst melden", off by default; profiles only): each profile decides in the "Ich" window under "Von selbst" whether and why the assistant speaks unasked, each kind on its own: appointments ahead ("In 20 minutes: dentist. Shall I remind you again at 9:55?", a "yes" sets the reminder), its own smart home rules ("Whirlpool above 37.5", "bathroom window is open and rain is on", "washing machine power below 5 for 3 minutes"; read only, never switching), a short greeting with the next appointment or reminder when the conversation is opened after three hours or more, at most one follow-up a day on a plan mentioned yesterday, new mail from chosen senders (sender and subject only) and a weather note for tomorrow via web search. Plus quiet hours, a daily limit and learning from feedback: "nicht jetzt" means two hours of quiet, "das interessiert mich nicht" halves how often that kind may speak (can be reset). The panel decides when, by fixed rules from real data; where the model words something (follow-up, weather), its answer only counts when it quotes the data. With the conversation open the note is spoken, otherwise it arrives as a push notification. Since V01.0.179 only the first device of the profile says it (page or iPhone app), the others stay silent, just like reminders (V01.0.176). Every note is in the log with its reason.

**Natural turn-taking** (on by default, conversation settings): during a short pause the recording is transcribed already. A finished sentence ends the turn at once and its transcript becomes the question, so the answer starts noticeably sooner; after "and …", "because …", "um" or a comma the assistant waits up to 2 s for you to go on.

**New conversation every day** (on by default, conversation settings → Display): the first question of a new day starts a new conversation; earlier ones stay in the history and can be picked and continued.

**Speaker identification** (off by default, Settings → Features): each profile reads three short sentences in the "Ich" window. When the assistant then clearly recognizes a voice, it answers for that profile, with its memory, documents, reminders and voice, even on a guest device. The conversation shows "🎙 name" below the question. When someone other than the logged-in profile speaks at a device, the assistant gets only that one question (not the device's conversation), and the exchange stays out of the logged-in profile's history and memory. When the voice is unclear, nothing changes. Recognition runs on the Spark's CPU (Resemblyzer model, Apache 2.0) and needs no GPU memory. Strictness is adjustable. A voice is not a password: someone who sounds very similar or plays a recording can get into a profile.

**Profiles and memory:** under Profiles the admin creates profiles with a name and PIN. In the chat you log in with the "Guest" button at the top of the conversation by typing name and PIN. Logged in, the assistant remembers things for good when you tell it to ("remember that I'm vegetarian") or when they will be useful later, and forgets them on request; the Me lists what it knows, for deleting. Memory and conversations belong to the profile and live on the Spark, so the same history shows on every device you are logged in on: which profile is asking is decided only by the login (cookie) or the device key, never by the model, and guests get no memory. Speakers and your own programs get a device key under Profiles that belongs to one profile, and send it as the header `X-Speech-Device` to `/api/chat`. A new PIN logs out every browser of that profile; deleting a profile removes its memory and devices. The data lives on the Spark under `/var/lib/speech-spark/users`, one folder per profile.

**Home Assistant** (Einstellungen → Funktionen → "Home Assistant", off by default): each profile connects its own Home Assistant in the "Ich" window under "Smart home" with the address (e.g. `http://homeassistant.local:8123`) and a long-lived access token (Home Assistant: your profile → Security; best for a separate non-admin user). Then "turn on the living room light" or "how warm is it in the bathroom?" work in the conversation. Commands go to Home Assistant's own Assist (`/api/conversation/process`), so only entities exposed to Assist (Settings → Voice assistants → Expose) can be switched. Devices Assist does not know are switched directly through their service (e.g. `switch.turn_on`), except locks, alarm systems and updates. With a **code word** ("Ich" window → Smart home) the assistant switches only when the code word is in the same message ("lights off, code word sunflower"); without it, it asks. The panel checks this itself, not the language model; the model and the stored conversations see only "[Codewort]", it is stored encrypted and compared with some tolerance for speech recognition (a letter or two off, "Apollo 13" = "Apollo dreizehn"). Questions need no code word. Also (ideas from ha-mcp): history ("how warm was it in the bathroom yesterday?"), several devices at once ("all lights in the living room off", each checked; an unknown room never switches the whole home), typo-tolerant device search and to-do lists such as the shopping list (read, add, tick off; changes need the code word). Questions about values, states, zones and where someone is are read directly (`/api/states`) instead: the assistant sees every device, sensor, zone and person the token's user may see, exposed or not. Home Assistant tells rooms (areas) only to an admin token; without admin rights devices are found by their names only. Only that profile can use its Home Assistant: guests never, and a voice recognized by speaker identification at someone else's device neither. The token is checked before saving, stays on the Spark (`users/<id>/homeassistant.json`, mode 0600) and is never sent back to the browser.

**Searching conversations**: the magnifier next to the conversation list (on phones in the menu) searches all earlier conversations, a profile's with a profile, the browser's as a guest. A click opens the conversation at that spot.

**Earlier conversations** (Einstellungen → Funktionen → "Frühere Gespräche", on by default; profiles only): the assistant can look up what you talked about before ("what did you tell me about the grill last week?", "what did we talk about yesterday?"), searching only your own profile's saved conversations. Conversations that have been quiet for ten minutes are also read once in the background, while nobody is talking, and up to three lasting facts per conversation go into the profile's memory, marked "automatic" in the "Ich" window, where they can be deleted. Each profile can switch this learning off in the conversation settings ("Learn from conversations"). Once a week (or with "Propose cleanup") the assistant proposes merging duplicate entries and removing outdated ones; nothing changes until you accept the proposal under Ich → Memory. With notifications on, a note is sent.

**Pronunciation:** Settings → Speech output → Pronunciation takes one rule per line, e.g. `DGX = De Ge Ix`. It applies to whole words and to all speech output, Open WebUI included.

**Barge-in:** while the assistant speaks, the microphone keeps listening. Talking for about a quarter of a second interrupts the answer, and what you said becomes the next question. This relies on the browser's echo cancellation; if the assistant interrupts itself on loud speakers, use headphones or untick "barge in" below the chat.

**Your own voice:** under Voices you can record a reference straight from the microphone (read the suggested text, about 10 seconds); the panel fills in the transcript with the speech recognition. Cloning needs a Base model (Settings → Speech output → model `…-Base`), then set the new voice as the default voice. A German reference gives much steadier German than the built-in speakers, which are not native German speakers. Under Voices, one or all of your own voices can be exported as a ZIP (reference audio and transcript) and imported again on this or another Spark; on a name clash the import renames, replaces or skips.

**Required tools and answer check (V01.0.117):** questions about appointments, mail, reminders, timers, weather, parcels, transit, lists, match results and news have to go through the matching tool (one table `NEED_TOOLS` in `chat.py`, also used by the quality test). While the model chooses the tool, the "tool choice" temperature (0.1) applies, the normal one afterwards. The answer check (Settings → Language model, on by default) lets no sentence through for such questions that names a time, date, score or amount that is in no tool result, the question or the conversation so far; instead the assistant says once "Genauere Angaben dazu habe ich nicht." If the model answers without looking anything up, the answer is dropped and the model is asked once more. Both show up in the tool log as "Antwort-Prüfung". General questions without a tool are not checked. Optional: the admin allows "think while choosing tools", each profile switches it on under Me → Conversation (off, never guests).

**Learning from corrections (V01.0.118):** admin switch Features → "Aus Korrekturen lernen" and a switch per profile (Me → Conversation), both off, never guests or another voice. When a message right after an answer starts like a correction ("Nein, …", "Das stimmt nicht", "Du sollst …", at least three words), the model words a short sentence on the side (`fixes.py`) and the assistant ends with "Soll ich mir merken: …?". It is stored only after a plain yes from the same conversation within 15 minutes. Never proposed: sentences about code words, PINs, passwords, rules, security, confirmations, locks or alarms, world knowledge (scores, amounts) and links; after mail or outside text there is no proposal, and the earlier answer is then not sent along. The tool log shows a "Korrektur" entry; "Send the question to the admin as a test case" puts the corrected question into `quality-cases.json` (at most 30) and it runs with the quality test: all tools offered, each finds nothing, made-up figures count as a failure. The admin deletes them under System and update → Quality test.

**Language model settings (V01.0.121):** Under Settings → Language model → Advanced the admin sets the history length (4000–64000 characters, default 24000), web searches per answer (1–3, default 2), the time limit for an answer (60–600 s), top_p and presence_penalty (0 = the server's default) and own keywords that require a tool (one line per tool, e.g. `web_search: elfmeter, aufstellung`; whole words only, at most 20 per tool, the built-in words always stay). "Restore default" fills in the shipped system prompt, "Show the whole prompt" lists all parts in order and marks the fixed ones. With Features → "Own conversation style" (off) each profile can say under Me → Conversation → Answer, in at most 500 characters, how the assistant should talk to it; the text sits below the system prompt and above the fixed rules, never applies to guests or another voice and cannot be changed with a device key. Fixed: tool rules, handling of outside text, code word, locks and the values of background jobs (learning, briefing, mail sorting, room).

## The panel

On the first admin login a **setup wizard** walks through password, language model (with a connection test), voice (to listen to), profiles, access and features, and ends with the live check. Start it again: *Status → Checks → Run setup again*.

The menu (since V01.0.145, design "Klar") is a sidebar on the left on computers and a bar at the bottom on phones (Assistent, Ich, Zustand, Verwalten). At the top are **Assistent** and **Ich** for yourself, below them under *Spark verwalten* (manage Spark) the admin pages. Zustand (status) and Einbinden have a small bar with sub-pages at the top; a number next to "Einstellungen" counts unsaved pages. On a computer "Ich" opens as a page right of the sidebar since V01.0.147 instead of a window; another menu entry closes it.

- **Assistent** (assistant): voice chat with the dgx-spark-qwen38 LLM. Speak (or type); the answer comes back as text and streamed speech, sentence by sentence while the LLM is still writing. Hands-free mode listens again after each answer; the space bar or "Speak" interrupts. If nobody says anything for 8 seconds after an answer, the microphone goes off and the status line says so ("Heard nothing, microphone off"); only "Hey Spark" listens all the time. **Follow-up without wake word** (admin: Features → Follow-up without wake word, off by default; profile: Me → Conversation, off/4/6/8/10 seconds): after answering a spoken question (via "Hey Spark" or "Speak") the microphone stays open for the chosen time, so "And tomorrow?" works without the wake word; if nothing comes it goes off and "Hey Spark" keeps listening. Unlike hands-free only briefly and only after spoken questions; never after typed questions, never for guests, never in room mode. The ESP32 speakers do not have it (they listen for "Jarvis" on the chip). On an iPhone, iOS ends the microphone when the screen locks, the page goes to the background, a call comes in or Siri speaks. The panel notices, says so, gets a fresh microphone the next time it listens and, in hands-free mode, listens again by itself once the page is visible. While iOS only holds the microphone (muted), that time does not count as silence.
- **Ignore my own voice** (admin: Features → Ignore my own voice, off by default, with "What it ignores" below; profile: Me → Conversation, on once the admin allows it): every device only filters its own echo. When the speaker answers and the iPhone or a browser next to it listens, the Spark voice used to arrive there as a new question. The Spark now remembers in memory for 60 seconds what it said on which device (at most 20 pieces, never on disk). A profile's recording whose word pairs come at least 60 % from those sentences counts as silence (browser: "Ignored my own voice.", iPhone: "Nichts verstanden.", speaker: diagnosis "Eigene Stimme überhört"). With "also everything while another device is speaking" (recommended), a recording that falls at least 70 % into the time another device of the same profile is speaking (plus 1 second) does not count either; the speaking device itself is never held back, so "Stopp" still works there. Sentences under three words ("Ja", "Stopp") and sentences starting with "Spark", "Jarvis" or "Computer" always count. Only for signed-in profiles (login, iPhone app, speaker), never for guests, so nobody can test what the Spark just said to someone else. The log shows only `chat: ignored (own voice)` or `(speaking pause)`, never the text. Home Assistant via Wyoming is not covered.
- **Zustand** (status; "Übersicht" until V01.0.144): On top one sentence on whether everything runs, below it "Braucht dich" (needs you: update ready, unsaved settings, a service off or failing) with a button to each place; the dot next to "Zustand" in the menu shows the same in green, yellow or red. qwen38 and GPU processes are folded. *Monitoring* (GPU load, free unified memory, temperature, CPU with history; per service status, requests, latency, RTF, start/stop/restart; the running qwen38 lane), *System and update* (version, update, backup, update log), *Checks* (live check, quality test, benchmark, try speech recognition and speech output) and *Logs*.
- **Einstellungen** (settings): sub-pages on the left. The menu has three blocks: *What it can do*, *How it thinks and speaks*, *The Spark itself*; above them a field searches all settings and guides (a hit opens the page and marks the spot, even below a switch that is off). The Me window has the same search field. First *Features*: one switch per capability, in groups (conversation, my day, mail and documents, home, on the go and devices, access). Each switch has one sentence, small marks (*set up under Me → …*, *never for guests*, *read only*, *leaves the house*) and a guide; its settings (e.g. the SearXNG address under web search, the Open-Meteo addresses under weather, the strictness under speaker identification) sit indented right below it and appear as soon as it is on. Since V01.0.171 each feature is first one short line (arrow, name, switch); tapping the arrow or name opens its sentence, marks, guide and settings, and switching it on or a search hit opens it by itself. On top, *All*, *On*, *Off* and *Needs you* filter the list (a feature that is on but misses something: SearXNG address, allowed addresses for Wyoming, Telegram token, Apple key, speaker firmware; its line then says "needs you"); the browser remembers the choice. Below, a bar jumps to the groups. Then *Language model* (LLM, advanced), *Defaults* (for guests and new profiles), *Speech output*, *Voices*, *Speech recognition* (Qwen3-ASR or Parakeet), *Security* (assistant without password, password, second step, API key, reverse proxy, home network) and *Operation* (ports, memory guard, watchdog, logs). Each sub-page saves on its own; the bar at the bottom shows unsaved changes (with "Discard"), and leaving the settings or the page asks first. Only services whose settings changed are restarted.
- **Profile und Geräte** (profiles and devices): profiles and devices with a key.
- **Einbinden** (integrate): *Guides* (for every service: what it does, what you need, setup, use, what leaves the house, switching off) and *Apps and interfaces* with values to copy for Open WebUI, other OpenAI-compatible apps, the Pebble watch, Siri, curl and Python.

The panel is available in English and German; the button at the top right switches (default: the browser's language).
